# Narrative-Driven Interface

Principles is not a dashboard. It is a thinking system that helps a person move through reality over time.

The interface should therefore read like a story, not a collection of modules.

## Product narrative

Every important experience should make this sequence visible:

1. **What do I want?** — the desired reality.
2. **What is true?** — the observed reality.
3. **What is in the way?** — the meaningful gap and problem.
4. **Why is it happening?** — diagnosis and root-cause hypothesis.
5. **What will I change?** — design of the machine.
6. **What will I do now?** — accountable execution.
7. **What happened?** — actual outcome versus expected outcome.
8. **What did I learn?** — reflection.
9. **What principle should survive this experience?** — reusable rule.

The user should be able to answer three questions within a few seconds of entering a surface:

- Where am I in the story?
- What changed reality to get me here?
- What deserves action now?

## Narrative hierarchy

### 1. Chapters, not cards

Use large spatial transitions to distinguish moments in the story. A chapter can change background, scale, density, or typography. Do not wrap every concept in a bordered container.

Cards are reserved for genuinely independent objects. They are not the default layout primitive.

### 2. Turning points deserve visual force

Reality, the active gap, the current decision, and the current action are turning points. They should receive more contrast and scale than metadata, navigation, or historical detail.

A user should not need to scan every section to discover the present tension.

### 3. Time is visible

Past, present, and future must feel different.

- Past: quieter, evidential, inspectable.
- Present: highest contrast and strongest action affordance.
- Future: visible enough to orient the user, but not competing with the present.

The 5 Steps are a storyline through time, not a progress bar for task completion.

### 4. Reality before action

The interface should visually reinforce the reasoning sequence:

**Dream → Reality → Gap → Diagnosis → Design → Do → Outcome → Reflection → Principle**

Do not lead with actions when the reality or problem is still undefined.

### 5. Expected versus actual is a first-class comparison

Execution is not success. Completing an action is not proof that a design worked.

When an outcome exists, expected and actual reality should be compared directly and visibly.

### 6. Principles emerge from lived experience

A principle is the epilogue of a cycle, not an isolated note. The interface should preserve the chain of evidence that produced it.

## Visual grammar

The reference direction is severe, editorial, and highly legible rather than decorative.

- **Charcoal / near-black** for orientation and moments of decision.
- **Ivory / warm paper** for reflection and evidence.
- **Signal red** for tension, current state, and irreversible attention.
- **Green** only for learned/validated progress, not generic success decoration.
- Strong sans-serif typography for declarations and turning points.
- Serif typography for reflection, explanation, and lived evidence.
- Large whitespace between chapters; tighter density inside an active working surface.
- Rules, lines, and numbered sequences are preferred over rounded cards and pills.

This direction can be inspired by the clarity and system-thinking associated with Ray Dalio's *Principles*, but the product must keep its own visual identity and must not reproduce another product's branded interface.

## Surface rules

### Me

This is the canonical narrative surface.

The active goal is a story about changing reality. Dream and Reality establish orientation; Gap creates tension; 5 Steps show the causal journey; Now is the working climax; Outcome and Reflection close the loop.

### Organization

Tell the story of organizational reality:

**Intent → Current structure → Tension → Responsibility → Decision → Consequence**

Do not present roles, members, and issues as unrelated inventories.

### Knowledge

Tell the story of inquiry:

**Question → What we know → Evidence → Tension / disagreement → Synthesis → Decision implication**

The answer should not be visually separated from the evidence that warrants it.

### Learning

Tell the story of accumulated experience:

**Experience → Reflection → Pattern → Principle → Evidence across cycles → Revision**

A principle library should reveal provenance and evolution, not just store statements.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

- dashboard grids as the default composition;
- repeated rounded cards;
- hero sections that behave like marketing pages;
- equal visual weight for every state;
- progress indicators that imply completion equals success;
- hiding Reality or the active tension below historical detail;
- decorative red accents with no semantic meaning;
- generic AI-generated gradients, glass panels, and pill-heavy navigation.

## Review test

Before shipping a surface, capture desktop and mobile screenshots and ask:

1. Can someone point to the present tension without reading everything?
2. Is the current action obviously the current action?
3. Does the page show how the current state emerged from prior reality?
4. Can a user distinguish evidence from interpretation from decision?
5. Does the screen feel like one unfolding story rather than several widgets?

If the answer to any of these is no, the surface is not yet narrative-driven.
